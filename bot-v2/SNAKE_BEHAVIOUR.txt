CODEHAMMER BOT-V2 SNAKE BEHAVIOUR
=================================

This document describes the behaviour implemented by bot-v2/main.cpp. The
values below are implementation rules, not future suggestions.

PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
-----------------

Keep several independently survivable dragons alive. Pearl collection is useful
only when it does not create a collision, a shallow pocket, or an excessively
long body. The bot prefers preserving racers over concentrating the team's
length in one dragon.

REPLAY FINDING THAT DRIVES THE SPLIT POLICY
-------------------------------------------

In the Big Empty replay from 2026-09-24, bot-v2 stopped splitting after round
116. Its nine deaths were eight self-collisions and one collision with another
body. The final dragon grew to length 174 and self-collided in round 481.

The former policy caused this failure in three ways:

- each dragon process could split only once;
- consolidation and endgame disabled splitting;
- every split removed only two cells, even from a very long parent.

The current policy removes all three limitations.

NON-NEGOTIABLE MOVEMENT SAFETY
------------------------------

1. Every turn emits a valid action.
2. Known kelp edges and visible occupied destinations are rejected.
3. Sprint routes are checked one step at a time, including every intermediate
   edge and destination.
4. The bot reconstructs and simulates its full visible body when possible. A
   collision is checked before the tail is released, matching engine order.
5. Each candidate is checked for open exits, two-ply continuation, reachable
   space, future tail access, visible heads, and turn-order head conflicts.
6. A confirmed collision or shallow-space trap outweighs every pearl reward.
7. Unknown portal exits and invisible destinations are last-resort options.

REGULAR LENGTH-CONTROL SPLITTING
--------------------------------

A dragon can split repeatedly. A parent waits at least 12 rounds between its
own splits, and a newly spawned child must survive six turns before it may
split. These delays prevent an uncontrolled same-round split cascade.

The preferred split thresholds are:

    opening        length 10
    expansion      length 14
    consolidation  length 16
    endgame        length 18

If the entire tail and child spawn area are visible, the bot may split at the
preferred threshold when:

- the team is below its map-size-aware racer target;
- the parent has at least two fully observed safe moves;
- the new tail-head has at least two exits; and
- both resulting dragons can retain at least four segments.

The child receives approximately half the parent's segments. For example,
length 24 becomes two length-12 racers. This is intentionally different from
the old fixed length-2 child, which barely reduced a long parent's collision
risk and created fragile children.

HARD LENGTH CEILING
-------------------

The preferred tail check is impossible after the tail leaves the 7x7 vision
window. To prevent that uncertainty from allowing another length-174 dragon,
the bot uses an emergency split threshold:

    opening        length 20
    expansion      length 22
    consolidation  length 20
    endgame        length 18

At or above this threshold, the bot may split even when the old tail is not
visible, provided the parent still has two safe moves. The child acts later in
the same round and evaluates its own fresh 7x7 view before moving. This trades
a bounded spawn uncertainty for much lower self-collision risk and another
independent racer.

The desired team size is:

    min(engine unit limit, clamp(ceil(map area / 256) + 1, 2, 12))

When the team is already at this limit, the engine forbids further splits.

LENGTH CONTROL WHEN A SPLIT IS BLOCKED
--------------------------------------

At or above the emergency threshold:

- immediate pearl value falls to a token value;
- long-range pearl attraction falls to 10% of normal;
- a safe sprint may spend one or two segments when its net growth is zero or
  negative; and
- the scoring system rewards this controlled shedding enough to prefer it over
  continuing to grow, while all ordinary collision checks still apply.

This fallback also works in endgame. Below the emergency threshold, endgame
still disallows sprinting so useful length is not discarded unnecessarily.

PEARL AND EXPLORATION PRIORITIES
--------------------------------

Short dragons value pearls most. Once a dragon reaches its preferred split
length, direct pearl value is capped and route-level pearl attraction falls to
45%. Once it reaches the emergency threshold, survival and length reduction
dominate.

Pearl scores never grant permission to enter a known trap. Confirmed pearls,
remembered pearls, sonar hints, spawn countdowns, unseen territory, stale
territory, exits, future space, and tail reachability are combined only after
the route passes physical movement validation.

TEAM RIGHT-OF-WAY AND SONAR
----------------------------

Status sonar reports a dragon's predicted position and capped length. A shorter
dragon yields near a longer teammate. Danger and pearl messages remain advisory
because sonar can be intercepted or imitated; direct vision always wins.

Longer dragons demand more reachable space and receive larger uncertainty and
head-proximity penalties. Shorter dragons can accept more exploration risk, but
no dragon knowingly chooses a proven collision.

EXPECTED EFFECT ON THE BIG EMPTY FAILURE
-----------------------------------------

Under the new rules, losses reopen a team slot and a surviving long dragon can
split again during consolidation or endgame. A length-100 survivor is divided
roughly into two length-50 racers rather than retaining one self-collision-prone
body or shedding only two cells. Repeated safe splits replenish the race until
the engine's unit limit is reached.
