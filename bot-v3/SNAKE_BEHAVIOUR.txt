CODEHAMMER BOT-V3 SNAKE BEHAVIOUR
=================================

bot-v3 is the attacker/survivor variant built on bot-v2's collision,
flood-space, sonar, tail-simulation, and length-control logic.

SMALL RANDOM SPLITS
-------------------

The requested child range is length 1 to 5. The engine defines MIN_SIZE as 2
and rejects any split that leaves either dragon shorter than 2. Bot-v3 therefore
uses the full legal range 2 to 5.

The split size is a reproducible random value based on dragon ID, round, and
parent length:

    child minimum = 2
    child maximum = min(5, parent length - 2)

Preferred split thresholds are length 7 in opening, 9 in expansion, and 10 in
consolidation/endgame. Hard thresholds for an unseen tail are 14, 16, 14, and
12 respectively.

A new child waits five turns before splitting. A parent waits eight rounds
between splits. The team target is map-size aware and capped at 12 or the engine
unit limit, whichever is lower.

The parent requires at least two safe moves. When the complete tail is visible,
the child head should also have two exits unless the hard threshold has already
been reached.

STABLE TEAM ROLES
-----------------

Roles remain fixed for a dragon's lifetime:

    ID divisible by 3  -> ATTACKER
    every other ID     -> SURVIVOR

A length-2 or length-3 attacker temporarily behaves conservatively until it
reaches length 4. Its assigned role does not change.

ATTACKER BEHAVIOUR
------------------

Attackers pursue visible or recently sonar-reported enemy heads. They prefer
pressure distance two, then distance three, with diminishing pursuit rewards
out to distance eleven. Distance one is penalised, and known head collisions
remain forbidden by the common safety scoring.

When an enemy is nearby, attackers halve pearl value so they do not abandon the
chase for food. They accept more possible sprint-head risk and place slightly
less weight on open space than survivors.

An attack means pressure and route denial. It never means deliberately entering
an enemy body, which would kill the attacker.

SURVIVOR BEHAVIOUR
------------------

Survivors preserve independent racers. They move away from enemy heads within
six tiles, value exits and flood-filled space more strongly, prefer tail access,
apply larger head-threat penalties, and yield more strongly to longer teammates.

LENGTH CONTROL
--------------

Pearl attraction decreases once a dragon reaches its preferred split length and
drops to a token value at the hard threshold. If splitting is blocked, a fully
checked sprint may spend one or two segments with non-positive net growth.

Splitting and this length-control behaviour remain available in every game
stage, including consolidation and endgame.

COMMON SAFETY RULES
-------------------

1. Known kelp and visible occupied destinations are rejected.
2. Every sprint step and intermediate destination is validated.
3. The visible body is simulated in engine order: collision, head insertion,
   pearl growth, tail release, then sprint cost.
4. Candidate moves are checked for exits, future moves, flood space, tail
   access, visible heads, turn order, and teammate conflicts.
5. Pearl value cannot outweigh a confirmed collision or shallow-space trap.
6. Unknown portal exits and invisible destinations are last-resort moves.

SONAR
-----

Status sonar reports predicted position and capped length. Short teammates yield
to longer teammates. Enemy status and danger messages provide short-lived role
targets, while direct current vision always overrides sonar.
