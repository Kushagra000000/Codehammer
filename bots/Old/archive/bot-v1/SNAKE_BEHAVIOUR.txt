CODEHAMMER SNAKE BEHAVIOUR
==========================

This document describes the behaviour implemented by mybot/main.cpp. It is not
an aspirational plan: the thresholds and priorities below match the code.

NON-NEGOTIABLE SAFETY RULES
---------------------------

1. Every turn emits an action. If every observed route is fatal, the bot still
   emits a syntactically valid move so it never dies merely from missing output.
2. Kelp edges are rejected.
3. Tiles containing any visible dragon segment are rejected. This includes the
   snake's own tail because collision is checked before the tail advances.
4. A higher-ID head that can still enter the planned final tile during this
   round receives a very large danger penalty. This normally outweighs a pearl.
5. Unknown portal exits are last-resort moves. Known but currently invisible
   exits are also strongly disfavoured when a fully observed move exists.
6. Sprint paths are checked step by step. The code never requests more than
   LENGTH - 1 steps and checks each intermediate edge and destination.
7. Sprint body simulation is deliberately conservative: body cells are not
   assumed to vacate during the sprint. This may reject a technically safe route
   through a departing tail, but it will not approve that route by mistake.

PERSISTENT MEMORY AND PATHFINDING
---------------------------------

Each snake process remembers, by absolute wrapped coordinate:

- whether and when a tile was seen;
- the most recently observed pearl and pearl countdown;
- all observed kelp and portal edges;
- both endpoints of portal pairs once discovered;
- short-lived pearl and danger hints received through sonar.

The bot runs wrapped breadth-first searches over this remembered map. Known kelp
is blocked. Known portal pairs teleport to the corresponding exit. An unpaired
portal is not used by long-range pathfinding. Unseen ordinary edges are treated
as explorable until direct vision proves otherwise.

MOVE PRIORITY
-------------

Legal plans are ranked using these factors:

1. Pearls eaten by the plan.
2. Sprint segment cost.
3. Distance to confirmed, remembered, sonar-reported, or imminently spawning
   pearls. Sonar and countdown targets are treated as weaker evidence.
4. Distance to unseen or stale territory.
5. Number of exits remaining at the final tile.
6. Head-to-head danger and recently reported enemy danger.
7. Continuing forward is a small tiebreak preference; reversing is penalised.
8. A deterministic ID/round tiebreak prevents identical snakes from always
   making the same choice.

Sprint candidates contain at most three steps. During the opening and expansion
stages, a sprint is allowed only when pearls eaten minus segments spent is at
least +1. During consolidation it must produce at least +2 net growth. Endgame
never sprints because preserving length and reducing collision risk matter more.

GAME STAGES
-----------

OPENING — rounds 1 to 125

- Strongest exploration bonus; rapidly builds topology and pearl memory.
- Takes profitable sprints with at least +1 net growth.
- May split a length-8-or-longer snake into a child of length 2.
- Splitting requires at least two fully observed safe exits.
- Team expansion stops at a map-size-aware target of 2 to 12 snakes.

EXPANSION — rounds 126 to 300

- Pearl routing becomes more important, while exploration remains useful.
- Profitable sprints still require at least +1 net growth.
- Splitting is more selective: length at least 12, a safe local position, and
  team size below the same map-size-aware target.

CONSOLIDATION — rounds 301 to 424

- Splitting stops completely.
- Open-space and escape-route scores become more important.
- A sprint is accepted only when it creates at least +2 net growth.
- Speculative pearl/countdown routes remain possible, but exploration is much
  less important than survival and reliable growth.

ENDGAME — rounds 425 to 500

- No splitting and no sprinting.
- Unverified sonar pearls and predicted pearl spawns are ignored by pathfinding.
- Confirmed pearls remain useful, especially for a short snake, but their score
  is reduced for an already-long snake.
- Escape routes and avoiding all head proximity receive their highest weights.
- The intended result is to preserve the longest living snake and team length.

SPLITTING
---------

The desired team size is based on map area:

    clamp(ceil(map area / 256) + 1, 2, 12)

Each process splits at most once. A split always creates a length-2 child and is
attempted only when Controller::can_split(2) confirms it is legal. Newly created
children run independent processes, build their own map memories, and can later
split once if they grow enough during an eligible stage.

SONAR
-----

The bot uses a packed unsigned 32-bit message with:

- a four-bit Codehammer marker;
- message type (pearl or danger);
- six bits each for x and y;
- the round modulo 512;
- a five-bit payload.

A nearby visible enemy head is reported before a pearl. Otherwise the nearest
visible pearl is reported. Received messages older than 40 rounds, outside the
map, or without the marker are ignored. Because enemies can intercept or imitate
sonar, received data is only a temporary hint and direct vision always overrides
it.

KNOWN CONSERVATIVE TRADE-OFFS
-----------------------------

- The bot does not assume an unseen portal landing tile is empty.
- It does not assume a tail will vacate during a sprint.
- Long-range pathfinding ignores moving dragon bodies because their locations
  become stale quickly; current visible occupancy is enforced for the action.
- Enemy multi-step sprint intentions cannot be known. The bot strongly protects
  against one-step head contests from dragons that act later in the round.
- Memory is per snake process. Sonar is the only cross-snake hint mechanism.
