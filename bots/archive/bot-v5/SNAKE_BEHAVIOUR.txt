CODEHAMMER BOT-V5 SNAKE BEHAVIOUR
=================================

bot-v5 refines the two-phase strategy observed in M145346. It keeps bot-v4's
minimum-size swarm and longest-snake transition, but adds strong first-phase
map dispersal, deliberate known-portal use, and a 25%/75% late-role split.

ENGINE SIZE LIMIT
-----------------

BattleDragon's legal minimum length is 2. Length 1 cannot be produced by a move
or split, so "one to two" is implemented as length 2 whenever possible.

PHASE 1: DISPERSED MAP-COVERING SWARM, ROUNDS 1-300
---------------------------------------------------

The team attempts to fill the engine's complete unit limit. A length-4 dragon
splits into two length-2 racers. Larger parents use a reproducible random child
length in the legal range 2 to 5:

    child minimum = 2
    child maximum = min(5, parent length - 2)

New processes wait two turns before splitting; a parent waits four rounds
between its own splits. The emergency split threshold is length 6 when the old
tail is outside vision.

Length-2 dragons value pearls normally so they can reach another split. At
length 3, pearl attraction decreases but still permits growth to 4. At length 4
or more, splitting takes priority. Once the unit limit is full, safe
non-growing sprints may spend surplus segments and move dragons back toward
length 2.

HIGH DISPERSAL COEFFICIENT
---------------------------

During phase 1, every move receives a teammate-spacing score based on visible
heads and fresh sonar positions:

- distance 1-2 from a teammate receives a very large penalty;
- distance 3-6 receives a decreasing separation penalty; and
- distance 7-16 receives an increasing dispersal reward.

Unseen-territory value is more than doubled, stale-territory value is doubled,
and exploration remains relevant even while a pearl target exists. The intended
result is a low-density swarm that covers separate regions rather than forming
clusters around the same pearls.

ACTIVE PORTAL USE
-----------------

During phase 1, each traversed known, paired portal adds a strong route bonus.
The bonus is comparable to a direct pearl and is applied only after the normal
step, occupancy, flood-space, and continuation checks.

An unpaired portal receives only a smaller exploration incentive because its
landing tile is unknown. It remains a last-resort route when a known safe move
exists. Once both portal endpoints are remembered, the bot deliberately uses
the portal to disperse and reach distant territory.

PHASE 2: SPECIALISTS, ROUNDS 301-500
------------------------------------

Late roles are deterministic and stable:

    dragon ID divisible by 4 -> GROWER  (approximately 25%)
    every other dragon       -> SUPPORT (approximately 75%)

Across IDs 0-63 this produces exactly 16 growers and 48 supports. Deaths may
change the living percentage slightly, but new split children receive roles by
the same rule.

GROWERS: BECOME THE LONGEST
----------------------------

Growers stop splitting completely after round 300. They also stop deliberate
length shedding and ignore combat-pursuit rewards.

Their long-range pearl attraction starts at 250% and increases with current
length. Direct pearl value starts at 45,000 and rises by 1,500 per segment for
scoring, capped at length 60. A grower considers a sprint only when the route
gains at least one net segment.

Growers use the more conservative space, tail-access, and head-threat weights.
Short teammates yield through length sonar, reinforcing established leaders.

SUPPORTS: STAY SMALL, YIELD, AND SABOTAGE
------------------------------------------

Supports continue the phase-1 length cycle after round 300:

- grow from length 2 toward a legal split at length 4;
- continue splitting small children while a unit slot exists;
- shed surplus length safely when the team is at the unit limit; and
- heavily yield near any longer teammate.

All late supports pressure visible or recently sonar-reported enemy heads. They
prefer a distance of two, then three, and receive diminishing pursuit value out
to distance eleven. They may occupy routes, contest space, and distract enemy
snakes, but they do not deliberately enter enemy bodies or accept a proven
head collision.

COMMON SAFETY RULES
-------------------

1. Known kelp and visible occupied destinations are rejected.
2. Every sprint step and intermediate destination is validated.
3. The visible body is simulated in engine order: collision, head insertion,
   pearl growth, tail release, then sprint cost.
4. Moves are checked for exits, continuation, flood space, tail access, visible
   heads, turn order, and teammate conflicts.
5. Pearl, portal, dispersal, and combat rewards cannot outweigh a confirmed
   collision or shallow-space trap.
6. Unknown portal landings and invisible destinations remain last-resort moves.

SONAR
-----

Status sonar reports predicted position and capped length. It supplies fresh
teammate positions for dispersal and right-of-way, and short-lived enemy targets
for support sabotage. Direct vision always overrides sonar because messages can
be intercepted or imitated.
